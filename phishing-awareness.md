Phishing Awareness Guide

1. Introduction

Phishing is a type of cybercrime used by criminals to try and obtain sensitive information from a target. This can be done with fake emails, texts, calls and websites.
Phishing can be a major issue when it comes to keeping a system secure.

2. Types of Phishing

Email Phishing
Emails sent that pretend to be from a trusted source like a bank, University or online service.

Spear Phishing
Targeted phishing attack towards a certain person or organisation. The attacker may use personal details to make the email seem more legitimate.

Smishing
This is phishing using SMS/text messages. Victoms may receive messages with links or ask for personal information.

Vishing
This is a type of phishing that uses fake calls. They will pretend to be from a trusted source.
3. Red Flags

Look out for the following:

Unusual requests for personal information
Suspicious links. Unfamiliar web address
Urgent messages. Threat to suspend your account
Unexpected Attachments
Spelling and grammatical issues
Requests for passwords, verification codes or banking details
Unusual email address

4. How to Prevent Phishing

Doing the following will help prevent becoming a victim of Phishing:
Check who the sender is.

Do not click on any suspicious links or unexpected attachments.

Go to the official website by typing the address into the browser.
Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on important accounts.
Use strong and unique passwords.
Ensure your OS, browsers and security are all up to date.
Never share your passwords or verification codes.
Report any suspicious messages to the organisation or IT department.
5. What to do if you receive a phishing message
If you receive a suspicious message:
Don’t click on any links or attachments.
Don’t give any personal or financial information.
Check the message via an official method.
Report the message to your Institutions IT or Security department.
Delete the message once it has been reported.
6. What to do if you become a victim of phishing
If you have interacted with a phishing message by accident:
Change your passwords using the official website.
Enable Multi-Factor Authentication.
Contact your bank immediately if any financial details may have been exposed.
Contact your Institutions IT department if you had a school/university account.
Ensure you run a suitable scan if you downloaded any suspicious files.
Keep an eye on your accounts for any strange activity.

7. Conclusion

Phishing can be a serious issue in the world of Cybersecurity as it targets human behaviour. Understanding the common methods of phishing and taking safe steps online can protect your personal information.

Cybersecurity should be taken as a team effort. Being aware is an important step to create a safer environment online.

Social Engineering Awareness

Introduction

Social engineering is a type of cyber attack designed to manipulate individuals into giving away sensitive information, gaining access to systems, or performing actions.

Rather than targeting a vulnerability in technology, it targets human behaviour, emotions and trust.

Common types of Social Engineering

Impersonation: An attacker will pretend to be a trusted person/employee/organisation/provider to gain sensitive information.

Pretexting: An attacker will create a plausible scenario/story to get you to give them sensitive information.

Baiting: An attacker will entice you to do something unsafe by offering something appealing e.g free software.

Tailgating: An unathorised person will follow someone with authorisation into a restricted area.

Scareware: False scare tactics are used to get users to download malware or give away information.

Why Social Engineering works

Social Engineering attacks often work because they target:

Trust in known organisations.
Fear of losing access to your account.
Curiosity.
Pressure to act fast.
Wanting to help colleagues or authority.
No awareness of cyber security.

Warning signs

Be wary if someone asks for:

Sensitive information.
To act quickly.
To bypass security processes.
For passwords or verification codes.
Unrealistic rewards.
Will not confirm their identity.
Prevention
Always confirm who they are.
Follow security processes.
Never give away passwords or authentication codes.
Question any unusual requests – even if coming from authority.

Do not connect any unknown storage.
Report any suspicious activity to the correct security team.
Attend Cyber Security Awareness training.
What to do if you think you are being targeted
Stop communication until you have confirmed what they want.
Don’t give away sensitive information.
Contact them via an independant source.
Report to the relevant IT / Security department.
Follow your organisations processes for reporting incidents.

Conclusion

Social Engineering shows that Cyber Security is not just a technical issue but also our responsibilty. By increasing awareness, confirming who they are and following security processes we can help protect ourselves and our organisation from being manipulated.

Cybersecurity Incident Reporting and Response

1. Introduction

A Cybersecurity incident is an incident which poses a threat to the confidentiality, integrity and/or availability of information systems, networks and data.

Examples of Cybersecurity incidents include: Phishing, Malware, Unauthorised Access, Data Breaches and Ransomware.

Having an effective incident response process will help organisations identify threats, reduce any damage and return to normal functioning.

2. Types of Cybersecurity Incident
Phishing
Messages sent to deceive users into providing sensitive information or doing unsafe things.
Malware
Malicious software which infects devices/systems.
Unauthorised Access
Someone gaining access to your account/network/system without permission.
Data Breach
Confidential/Sensitive information being accessed without permission.
Ransomware
Malicious software preventing you from accessing information/systems until payment is made.
3. How to report a Cybersecurity Incident
If you become aware of any suspicious Cybersecurity incidents do the following:
1. Identify the suspicious activity 2. Note the date and time of the incident 3. Report the incident to the correct IT/Security team 4. Give a detailed explanation of what has happened 5. Save any evidence that may be relevant 6. Follow any instructions given by authorised security staff.
Do not investigate or alter the affected systems unless authorised.
4. Incident Response
Preparation:
Create policies and provide training to staff. Create response protocols and ensure the correct security tools are in place.
Detection and Analysis:
Identify any suspicious activity. Analyse any evidence available to understand what type of incident has occurred.
Containment:
Ensure the incident is contained by taking the correct actions.
Eradication:
Identify and eliminate the cause of the issue, such as Malware.
Recovery:
Get systems back up and running safely. Monitor systems to ensure the threat is dealt with.
Lessons Learned:
Record the incident. Review how the incident was handled and identify any improvements which can be made to prevent similar incidents.

6. Why report incidents?

Reporting incidents is important as it:

Allows Security teams to investigate threats Minimises any potential damage to the organisation Helps preserve evidence Increases security awareness within the organisation Helps identify any vulnerabilities within security Allows accountability and helps prevent future incidents.

7. Conclusion

Reporting and responding to Cybersecurity incidents is an essential part of security within an organisation. Having a structure in place to respond to incidents will allow the organisation to detect, contain and recover from any security incidents whilst minimising any potential impact.

It is everyone’s responsibility to report any suspicious activities in a timely manner and follow security protocols.
